# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Individual teachers running live quizzes with classes of 20–35 students. They find blurt on their own, give it about a minute to decide whether it is worth trying with tomorrow's class, and then run it from a laptop: the room on a projector or classroom screen, every student on their own phone.

Students are the teacher's users. They never make an account: a room code and a first name are all they give, and the phone is a buzzer, not a place they read.

## Product Purpose

blurt is a live classroom quiz built around one mechanic: each question opens with its choices hidden, and the first student to claim the floor says the answer out loud. The teacher judges it right or wrong; only then do the choices appear for everyone. Recall first, recognition second.

Success is a teacher running a real lesson with it, and then seeing in the report which questions the room actually did not know.

## Brand Personality

Loud, quick, game-show. An 80s television studio with the lights down: neon tubes in pink and cyan, sign lettering, a light-bulb scoreboard. Confident and a little theatrical on the wall the class watches; quiet and fast on the teacher's screen, where someone is holding a room and a stopwatch at once.

The emotional goal for a student is the jolt of knowing it before anyone else. For the teacher it is control: the room moves when they press Space.

## Anti-references

- Anything resembling Kahoot, in look or in name. The word stays out of the build entirely, including marketing.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the real room.** Marketing and help use the actual app components, not illustrations or mock-ups of them.
2. **Students first.** A student who lands anywhere public is one step from typing their room code.
3. **The mechanic is the pitch.** Lead with "Know it? BLURT it!", not a feature list.
4. **Teacher time is the budget.** Anything a teacher needs to decide or act fits one scroll or one keypress.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA across every surface, in both the dark and light themes: contrast, keyboard operation, screen-reader labels, and `prefers-reduced-motion` respected for every animation. Accented names (Patiño, Łukasz, Nguyễn) are the normal case, not an edge case.
